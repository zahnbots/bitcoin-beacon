Wall Street Found a Way to Profit From a Flat Bitcoin

The coin barely moved this week. Two of the biggest names in finance still found a trade — by selling its swings to everyone else.

By The Bitcoin Beacon · OPINION · August 17, 2026 · 4 min read
The Take · Illustration: The Bitcoin Beacon

Bitcoin has spent the month going almost nowhere. It sat near $63,000 this week, down a few percent, boxed into a corridor so tight that options desks trade against every twitch. A quiet tape usually means a quiet week for the people who sell bitcoin products. This week it was the opposite.

Goldman Sachs agreed to pay up to $2.25 billion for the biggest bitcoin covered-call fund, a product that converts the coin’s volatility into a monthly check. Cboe filed to list the first 3x leveraged bitcoin ETF, a product that triples the coin’s daily move. One sells the swings as income. The other sells them as leverage. Neither holds a bitcoin.

The business is the motion, not the coin

That is the tell. When bitcoin’s price stalls, the value on the trading floor is no longer in owning it — it is in packaging its movement and renting that movement out. A covered-call fund harvests premium from bitcoin’s jumps and pays a yield near 27%; a 3x fund magnifies the jumps for traders who want a bigger bet. Both are engines that run on volatility, not on the asset. The coin can sit perfectly still and the fees still print.

These products are long bitcoin’s volatility and short its point.

The honest case for them is real, so state it plainly. These wrappers add liquidity, give cautious investors a regulated on-ramp, and let people express a view without wiring money to an exchange. Access is a genuine good, and for a retiree who wants a yield tied to bitcoin without babysitting a hot wallet, an income fund is a reasonable choice.

But look at where the risk settles. The covered-call fund capped its holders’ upside and still fell about 43% over the past year. Leveraged 2x crypto funds — the tamer cousins of what Cboe just filed — have lost as much as 96%. In every case the institution collects a fee that does not depend on the outcome, and the customer absorbs the decay. The house is long the volatility; the guest is long the loss.

What the wrappers can’t give you

None of this touches the reason bitcoin exists. A covered-call ETF cannot be sent to a relative in Lagos for a fraction of a cent. A 3x futures product cannot be held through a bank freeze or a currency collapse. They are claims on a strategy, custodied and hedged several steps from a coin no holder controls. They are bets on the price, dressed as ownership of the thing.

Russia spent this week deciding to let its citizens buy actual bitcoin, capped and monitored but real. Wall Street spent the same week deciding the coin is most profitable when you never have to touch it. Both are forms of adoption. Only one of them ends with a person holding their own money. When the price is loud, the difference is easy to miss. In a flat week like this one, it is the whole story.

The cheapest, most durable way to own bitcoin is still the oldest one: hold the coin.
