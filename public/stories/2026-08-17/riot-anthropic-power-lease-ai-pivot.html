Riot Leases Its Power to Anthropic for 20 Years

The bitcoin miner will hand 191 megawatts to an AI company through 2048 — a $9.1 billion bet that renting power beats mining coins.

By The Bitcoin Beacon · ROCKDALE, TEXAS · August 17, 2026 · 5 min read
Network & Mining · Illustration: The Bitcoin Beacon

Riot Platforms built its business on turning cheap Texas electricity into bitcoin. Its newest deal turns that same electricity into rent. The company will lease 191 megawatts of capacity at its Rockdale, Texas campus to Anthropic under a 20-year agreement running through June 2048, worth about $9.1 billion — and up to $16.1 billion if two five-year extension options are exercised.

Riot disclosed the contract alongside its second-quarter earnings on August 10 without naming the tenant; Bloomberg reported the customer was Anthropic. The capacity comes online in stages: 96 megawatts by December 2027, the full 191 by June 2028. In pre-market trading the day the market grasped the deal, Riot’s stock jumped roughly 20%.

Why a miner becomes a landlord

The logic is in Riot’s own cost sheet. Its direct cost to mine a single bitcoin rose to $49,912 in the second quarter, against a coin trading in the low-$60,000s — a shrinking margin that swings with price, difficulty and the four-year halving. A two-decade lease to a well-capitalized AI company pays a steadier bill, indexed to nothing more volatile than a tenant’s appetite for compute.

That trade — predictable rent over lottery-ticket block rewards — is now the defining move of the public mining sector. It reframes Riot less as a miner than as an energy-infrastructure company that happens to own bitcoin rigs.

The public miners’ most valuable asset is no longer a warehouse of ASICs. It is a grid connection.

The grid is the real prize

What AI hyperscalers cannot conjure is power, and that is exactly what miners spent a decade securing. Texas has frozen roughly 474 gigawatts of pending data-center grid-connection requests, which means Riot’s already-approved interconnection queue is a moat that newcomers cannot buy at any price. A trillion-dollar AI lab earns far more revenue per megawatt than an ASIC ever will, so when the two bid for the same electrons, the miner loses the auction — unless it becomes the landlord instead.

Riot is not alone. Keel Infrastructure sold 1,085 bitcoin for about $75 million to retire debt and fund a new 96-megawatt data center in Quebec, and Hyperscale Data liquidated 685 coins for roughly $43 million to finance a computing facility in Michigan. Across the sector, coins are being converted into concrete and cooling.

What it means for bitcoin

For the network, the pivot cuts two ways. Hashrate has kept climbing to record highs even as some operators redirect power, so bitcoin’s security is not in immediate danger. But the marginal megawatt is increasingly worth more to AI than to mining, and that pressure does not relent. If the most efficient, best-connected operators find they earn more hosting models than securing blocks, the long-run question is who keeps mining when the grid itself has a higher bidder.

Why it matters: the companies built to convert energy into bitcoin are discovering their grid connections are worth more than their coins — and acting on it.
