Citi Sets a Date to Custody Bitcoin

Citi says it will hold bitcoin for institutional clients by year-end on its new Custody+ platform, putting bitcoin on the same shelf as stocks and bonds.

By The Bitcoin Beacon · NEW YORK · August 19, 2026 · 5 min read
Markets & Institutions · A global custodian bank opens a bitcoin drawer · Illustration: The Bitcoin Beacon

Citi has put a clock on it. The bank told the market on August 18 that it expects to launch a bitcoin custody service for institutional clients later this year — its first digital-asset custody offering, and the concrete follow-through on an intention it first flagged last October.

The service will live inside Custody+, a new platform from Citi Investor Services built for a market drifting toward continuous trading and faster settlement. The pitch to institutions is not another wallet. It is one custody framework that holds their Treasuries, their equities and their bitcoin side by side, under a single global custodian they already use.

That framing is the whole story. Citi is not trying to out-feature a crypto-native custodian; it is offering something the startups can’t — the balance sheet, the regulatory standing and the operational reach of a globally systemic bank. For a pension fund or asset manager, the appeal of bitcoin custody at Citi is less about a new way to hold the asset and more about not having to hold it somewhere unfamiliar.

The plumbing pitch

Citi leaned on operational numbers rather than crypto enthusiasm. More than 80% of its custody event volume now processes in real time, the bank said, and it has cut the processing time of certain U.S. corporate actions by up to 92%. Custody+ is meant to extend that speed to a world where settlement no longer waits for business hours — the environment bitcoin has always lived in.

What Citi did not give was a firm launch date, a list of which other assets might follow bitcoin, or the size of the client demand behind the move. Executives have said the offering has been in development for two to three years, and that a 2026 target was set internally back in October 2025. This week’s news is the timeline hardening, not a product going live.

The startups sold custody as a feature. Citi is selling it as a bank you already trust.

The quiet trade-off

There is a tension worth naming. Every time a mega-custodian offers bitcoin, the asset gets easier for institutions to own — and a little more of its supply pools behind a handful of very large intermediaries. Bitcoin was designed to remove trusted third parties; a market where the biggest banks custody an outsized share of institutional coins is a different shape than the one its whitepaper imagined.

The counterpoint is that this is simply what adoption by large pools of capital looks like. Institutions will not self-custody billions in bitcoin; they will use a custodian, and a regulated bank is a more accountable one than most. Citi’s move doesn’t change what bitcoin is. It changes who is comfortable holding it — and that list just got materially longer.

Why it matters: custody is where institutional intent becomes an actual position. When a globally systemic bank puts bitcoin on the same shelf as government bonds, owning it stops being a fringe operational decision.
