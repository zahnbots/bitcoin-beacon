The Take · Opinion

The ETF Owns Bitcoin. The Phone Spends It.

Today’s headlines came from downtown: a cheaper ETF conversion, a 3%-bitcoin portfolio, a bill to define the asset. All real, all legitimizing — and all about bitcoin the asset. The adoption that answers the original question, money for people the banks skipped, was somewhere else. On Lightning, fees fall from 6–10% to a fraction of a cent. In Kenya, forty million M-Pesa numbers quietly became Lightning addresses this year; Machankura moves sats over USSD on phones with no internet. The two aren’t enemies — institutional demand supports a price that makes saving in bitcoin worth it for a Nairobi trader as much as a Toronto retiree. But when the day’s biggest bitcoin stories are all about who holds it, remember who uses it. Wall Street decides what bitcoin is worth. A market stall in Lagos decides what it’s for. Watch the till, not just the ticker.
