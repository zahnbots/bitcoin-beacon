The Take · Opinion

Wall Street Keeps Repackaging Bitcoin. The Coin Doesn’t Change.

Three stories this week rhyme. A hard fork sends compliance desks scrambling over which chain counts as “real” bitcoin. The SEC spins up a unit to police how companies book their coins. And in Seoul, leveraged single-stock funds made a national stock index swing harder than bitcoin itself. Different headlines, one pattern: the machinery of finance keeps wrapping bitcoin in new layers — trusts, proxies, derivatives, accounting rules. Each layer adds access, and a little distance. Let me steelman it, because the case is strong: financialization is what legitimacy looks like: a retiree holds bitcoin in a brokerage, a treasurer books it with an auditor’s sign-off, regulated custody means a stolen laptop is not a life savings gone. But a wrapper is a promise. When a fork hands out free coins, the self-custody holder simply has them; the ETF investor owns a claim on a trust whose sponsor decides whether the forked asset is even bitcoin. Same event, opposite experience. The coin at the center is unchanged — 21 million, final settlement, yours only if you hold the keys. Everything built around it is someone else’s to revoke.
