On Isla Mujeres, Sixteen Shops Now Take Bitcoin

Two years ago the count was zero — now a one-woman project is turning a Cancún ferry island into a Lightning economy.

ISLA MUJERES — On a limestone island a ferry ride from Cancún, you can now buy a taco, a hotel night, or a golf-cart tour with bitcoin. Two years ago you could not. BTC Isla, a not-for-profit started by Isabella Santos after she moved to the island in early 2024, has onboarded sixteen merchants to take payment over the Lightning Network — up from zero — and put an ATM and point-of-sale kiosks on the ground. It charges no fees. It runs free classes in a Bitcoin Café, each paired with a welcome dinner. Most of the sixteen are food-and-drink vendors, but the list now reaches lodging, transport, and tours. Sixteen is small against several hundred island businesses; the slope is the story. This is a hyper-local circular economy where wages and spending stay in sats, nudging the boundary of the fiat economy outward one merchant at a time.

Why it matters: the adoption that lasts is built shop by shop, not decreed from a capital.

The Take · Opinion

The State Found a Cheaper Way to Own Bitcoin

Kazakhstan won’t buy bitcoin. It will take a tenth of what its miners produce. Oman pooled its miners; Pakistan is wiring surplus power to its own rigs. Call it what it is: not conviction, but a royalty. A government that extracts coins can also switch the pipeline off — which is exactly why a mined reserve is weaker than a bought one.

Policy & Nation-States

Kazakhstan Takes a 10% Cut of Miners’ Bitcoin — ASTANA — A law that took effect August 1 requires the country’s largest bitcoin mines — those over 150 megawatts — to transfer 10% of what they mine each month to a state fund, which routes the coins into a national reserve. In return, miners get capped power on ten-year contracts. It is a mining royalty, not a sovereign conviction buy — and a future government can shut it off. Full story →

Markets & Institutions

Ninety Wallets Now Hold 10,000 Bitcoin Each — The number of wallets holding at least 10,000 BTC has climbed to 90, a six-month high, up six in eight weeks. Those addresses added a net 46,420 BTC over two months, the fastest accumulation of 2026, per Santiment — even as smaller holders sold after the Coldcard exploit and the CLARITY Act delay. Supply is rotating to stronger hands. Full story →

Money & Macro

Argentina Lets Its Banks Hold Bitcoin — BUENOS AIRES — Under a central-bank framework that reverses a 2022 ban, regulated Argentine banks can now offer bitcoin trading and custody inside the apps millions already use — through separate units held to higher capital and liquidity rules. In a country scarred by inflation, bitcoin moves from the blue-dollar street market toward the bank branch. Full story →

Network & Mining

MARA Borrows $600 Million Against Its Bitcoin — FORT LAUDERDALE — Rather than sell, the miner MARA pledged 18,750 BTC — about $1.2 billion — as collateral for $600 million in new debt from Coinbase and Two Prime. It is a bet the treasury out-earns the interest, and a tell about the moment: even as some firms dump coins, the largest miners are borrowing to keep theirs. Full story →
