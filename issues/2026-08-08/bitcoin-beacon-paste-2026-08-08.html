South Africa’s Bitcoin Township Takes Its Model Across Africa

MOSSEL BAY, South Africa — In a township above this Garden Route resort town, a surf coach collects his wages in bitcoin and buys his bread with it at a shop that takes the same coins. That loop is the whole idea behind Bitcoin Ekasi, which has paid its staff entirely in satoshis since 2021. This month it put the model on the road, launching a Pan-African Bitcoin Tour to link the continent’s other circular economies.

It is no longer alone. The Federation of Bitcoin Circular Economies now counts more than 37 community projects across at least a dozen African countries — from Zambia’s Bitcoin Campus to Malawi’s Women of Satoshi Cooperative — and a January summit tallied roughly 700 merchants taking daily bitcoin payments. Underneath runs Machankura, moving bitcoin over Lightning on basic feature phones, no smartphone or data required.

Why it matters: adoption that sticks is measured in wages paid and groceries bought, not coins bought and held.
