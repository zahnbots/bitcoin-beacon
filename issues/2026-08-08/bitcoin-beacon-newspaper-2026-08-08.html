South Africa’s Bitcoin Township Takes Its Model Across Africa

Bitcoin Ekasi pays its township staff in satoshis. Now it is on a pan-African tour — one node in a federation of 37 circular economies.

MOSSEL BAY, South Africa — In a township above this Garden Route resort town, a surf coach collects his wages in bitcoin and buys his bread with it at a shop that takes the same coins. That loop is the whole idea behind Bitcoin Ekasi, which has paid its staff entirely in satoshis since 2021. This month it put the model on the road, launching a Pan-African Bitcoin Tour to link the continent’s other circular economies.

It is no longer alone. The Federation of Bitcoin Circular Economies now counts more than 37 community projects across at least a dozen African countries — from Zambia’s Bitcoin Campus to Malawi’s Women of Satoshi Cooperative — and a January summit tallied roughly 700 merchants taking daily bitcoin payments. Underneath runs Machankura, which moves bitcoin over Lightning on basic feature phones, no smartphone or data required. The economies are small, donor-seeded and exposed to volatility. But where remittances are dear and bank branches scarce, paying a wage and settling a shopkeeper without a middleman is a real alternative, not a slogan.

Why it matters: adoption that sticks is measured in wages paid and groceries bought, not coins bought and held — and that is happening in more African towns than a year ago.

The Take · Opinion

Governments Learned to Mine Bitcoin. Now They Want the Pool.

Oman just ordered every licensed miner into a single state pool. Set it beside Kazakhstan’s accredited pool and tithe, Russia’s regional bans, Bhutan’s hydro-mining and Pakistan’s two-gigawatt allocation, and the pattern is clear: governments went from banning bitcoin mining to owning it.

Fine — hashrate defends the network whoever runs the machines, and regulated beats banned. The catch is one word: mandatory. A voluntary pool a miner can leave in seconds; a national one it cannot leave without leaving the country. That compulsion is exactly what bitcoin was built to deny. Watch pool concentration, not flags.

Policy & Nation-States

MUSCAT — Oman launched OmanHash, a national bitcoin mining pool, and made it mandatory: every licensed miner must route its hashrate through the state platform. The pool consolidates about 10 EH/s in phase one and was built by the same firm behind Kazakhstan’s state pool. A voluntary pool you can leave; a licence condition you cannot. Full story →

Markets & Institutions

NEW YORK — America’s spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in roughly $626 million over the first three trading days of August, and by one count have not posted a single day of net selling all month. BlackRock’s IBIT took about 76% of it — steady, sizable demand, routed almost entirely through one company. Full story →

PARIS — A cluster of European listed firms is racing to become the continent’s Strategy. Paris-listed Capital B has stacked about 3,000 BTC, with Germany’s Bitcoin Group and France’s Sequans close behind.
