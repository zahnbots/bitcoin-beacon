Russia Bans Bitcoin Mining Around Moscow Until 2032

MOSCOW — Russia is switching off the bitcoin miners on its capital's doorstep. A government decree signed July 25 extends a 2024 mining ban to the city of Moscow, its surrounding region and parts of Kursk, running from August 15 through the end of 2032.

The reason is the grid: officials count more than 65 data centres drawing 734 megawatts around Moscow, and a state power commission wanted the load gone. A similar Siberian ban trimmed more than 300 megawatts and, the energy ministry says, spared households from winter cuts.

Why it matters: a ban doesn't retire miners, it relocates them — and with Russia near a sixth of world hashrate, grid politics now decides where the network's security lives.

Minnesota Bans Bitcoin ATMs Statewide

The state switched off cash-to-crypto kiosks on August 1 after scammers used them to take nearly $1 million from residents, most of them seniors. Operators must cash customers out and remove the machines by year's end. It's the third state to ban them, after Tennessee and Indiana. Full story →
