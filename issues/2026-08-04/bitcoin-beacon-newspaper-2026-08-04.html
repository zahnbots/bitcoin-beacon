Russia Bans Bitcoin Mining Around Moscow Until 2032

A new decree switches off the miners on the capital’s doorstep to spare a strained grid — and reshuffles the world’s third-largest hashrate base.

MOSCOW — Russia is switching off the bitcoin miners on its capital’s doorstep. A decree signed July 25 extends a 2024 mining ban to the city of Moscow, the surrounding region and parts of Kursk, and runs from August 15 through the end of 2032.

The reason is the grid. Officials count more than 65 data centres drawing 734 megawatts around Moscow, and a state power commission wanted the load gone. Russia has done this before: a Siberian ban trimmed more than 300 megawatts and, the energy ministry says, spared households from winter cuts.

What a ban cannot do is destroy the hashing. Mining is the most portable heavy industry on earth — the rigs are light, the leases are short, and hash flows to the cheapest idle power. Russia sits near a sixth of the world’s mining; a Moscow blackout reshuffles that map rather than shrinking it.

Why it matters: bans don’t retire miners, they relocate them — and grid politics now decides where the network’s security lives.

The Take · Opinion

Bitcoin’s Newcomers Keep Getting Robbed at the Door

Minnesota just banned bitcoin ATMs after scammers used them to drain nearly a million dollars from residents. The same month, a firmware bug bled more than $100 million out of self-custody wallets. The pattern is old: the most dangerous mile in bitcoin is a beginner’s first one — buying a coin and learning to keep it. “Not your keys” is a slogan, not a consumer-protection policy. The movement that sells sovereignty owes first-timers guardrails, not just warnings.
