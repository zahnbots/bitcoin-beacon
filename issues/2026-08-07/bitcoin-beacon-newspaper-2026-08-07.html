A New Bitcoin Fork Will Airdrop Coins to Every Holder

Around August 21, a Drivechain project called eCash copies the entire Bitcoin ledger 1:1 — and drags every ETF, treasury, and custodian into a decision.

BLOCK 964,000 — Around August 21, at that block height, developer Paul Sztorc will split the Bitcoin chain and hand every holder a matching pile of a new coin called eCash. Hold 4.19 BTC, receive 4.19 eCash. Nothing you own moves; a copy of the ledger forks off and goes its own way.

The mechanics are old — Bitcoin Cash did this in 2017 — but the room has changed. The biggest holders are no longer hobbyists. Strategy sits on 818,334 coins; public companies hold roughly 1.2 million between them; spot ETFs custody more than a million, most of it at a single firm.

Each now faces a forced choice: claim the airdrop and trigger a taxable event auditors must disclose, or ignore free coins and explain that too. The fork also quietly reassigns up to 600,000 dormant coins linked to Satoshi to its own backers — a move critics call theft. For ordinary holders, the hazard is simpler: scammers are already circling the “free airdrop.”

Why it matters: most forks die, but this one forces Wall Street to decide what counts as bitcoin.

The Take · Opinion

Wall Street Keeps Repackaging Bitcoin. The Coin Doesn’t Change.

Three stories this week rhyme. A fork sends compliance desks scrambling over which chain is “real” bitcoin. The SEC spins up a unit to police how companies book their coins. And in Seoul, leveraged single-stock funds made a national stock index swing harder than bitcoin itself. Different headlines, one pattern: finance keeps wrapping bitcoin in new layers — trusts, proxies, derivatives, accounting rules. Each wrapper adds access and a little distance. The thing at the center is unchanged: 21 million, final settlement, yours only if you hold the keys.
