A New Bitcoin Fork Will Airdrop Coins to Every Holder

BLOCK 964,000 — Around August 21, at that block height, developer Paul Sztorc will split the Bitcoin chain and hand every holder a matching pile of a new coin called eCash. Hold 4.19 BTC, receive 4.19 eCash. Nothing you own moves; a copy of the ledger simply forks off and goes its own way.

The mechanics are old — Bitcoin Cash did this in 2017 — but the room has changed. The biggest holders are no longer hobbyists. Strategy sits on 818,334 coins; public companies hold roughly 1.2 million between them; spot ETFs custody more than a million, most of it at a single firm. Each now faces a forced choice: claim the airdrop and trigger a taxable event auditors must disclose, or ignore free coins and explain that too.

Why it matters: most forks die, but this one forces Wall Street to decide what counts as bitcoin.
