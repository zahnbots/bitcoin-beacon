Kazakhstan Claims a Tenth of Its Miners’ Bitcoin

A rule in force since Aug. 1 diverts a slice of every approved miner’s output into a state reserve — a country buying bitcoin by taxing the machines it powers.

ASTANA — Kazakhstan has started collecting bitcoin from the miners it powers. Under Government Resolution No. 638, in force since Aug. 1, approved “strategic” miners must hand 10% of their net mined coins each month to a state fund that routes them to the National Bank’s investment arm.

The bar is high by design: a qualifying site needs at least 150 MW of capacity and rigs clearing 150 TH/s each, and in return gets regulated grid access. President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev signed the enabling decree on July 7. But the reserve’s mandate stretches past bitcoin to crypto derivatives and company equity — a diversified state portfolio, not a pure coin stack.

The Take

The Register Says Bitcoin. The Settlement Says Dollars.

The Global South’s payment boom — Manila remittances, Nairobi tills, a miner’s tap-to-pay — runs on apps that turn bitcoin into local cash the instant it lands. Fees fall, which is real. But a rail nobody holds a coin on is bitcoin as plumbing, not money: custodial, convertible, and one regulator’s notice from off. Rails first, holders later is the bet. It is still only a bet. On the Ground

MANILA — Filipinos working abroad can now wire money home over the Lightning Network. Pouch.ph and Neutronpay have opened corridors from Canada and Vietnam that settle to Philippine banks and e-wallets in seconds, at better rates than the banks. The recipient sees pesos, never a coin — bitcoin as the invisible rail under a $38 billion-a-year remittance flow. Full story →

Network & Mining

ADDIS ABABA — Ethiopia has stopped issuing new bitcoin-mining permits, saying its grid has run out of room. The continent’s biggest mining hub had allocated about 600 MW across some two dozen firms and pulled roughly 2.5% of global hashrate from its hydro dams. The freeze caps growth by physics, not politics — and pushes the next rigs toward the neighbors. Full story →

Markets & Institutions

MANAMA — Gulf International Bank — owned by GCC governments led by Saudi Arabia — disclosed a $2.27 million stake in Strategy, the world’s largest corporate bitcoin holder, in its latest 13F. It is a rounding error, and the bank trimmed 365 shares last quarter. The direction, not the size, is the story. Full story →

What We’re Watching

Kazakhstan’s first haul. The reserve’s opening monthly transfer — how many coins the 10% rule actually yields — is due to surface in filings. Pouch’s next corridors. Manila’s remittance rail works only if the sending countries multiply; watch for Gulf and U.S. routes. Ethiopia’s spillover. Frozen out at home, container miners are shopping neighboring grids from Kenya to Zambia. Adopting Bitcoin, Cape Town. The African circular-economy summit lands Jan. 29 — the on-the-ground counterweight to the treasury conferences.
