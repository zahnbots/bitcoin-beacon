Kazakhstan Claims a Tenth of Its Miners’ Bitcoin

ASTANA — Kazakhstan has started collecting bitcoin from the miners it powers. Under Government Resolution No. 638, in force since Aug. 1, approved “strategic” miners must hand 10% of their net mined coins each month to a state fund that routes them to the National Bank’s investment arm. The bar is high by design: a qualifying site needs at least 150 MW of capacity and rigs clearing 150 TH/s each, and in return gets regulated grid access. President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev signed the enabling decree on July 7. But the reserve’s mandate stretches past bitcoin to crypto derivatives and company equity — a diversified state portfolio, not a pure coin stack.

Why it matters: a government is accumulating bitcoin by taxing the hashrate on its soil rather than buying on the open market.
