A Peruvian Charity Now Runs Its Aid on Bitcoin

CUSCO, Peru — The program that now moves bitcoin through fifteen Peruvian towns started with a pair of shoes. In 2019 a small charity called Motiv began fitting Andean children with boots sturdy enough for the walk to school. Six years on it reaches more than 750 families a week, teaches bitcoin six days a week across fifteen locations, and logged 25,000 to 30,000 bitcoin transactions from its 2025 events alone.

The pivot was an accident. In 2020, with donors refusing to redirect an earmarked shoe budget toward pandemic food, a bitcoin holder offered to fund it — on one condition: everything had to be bought in bitcoin. Motiv found merchants, kept a list, and taught them bitcoin as plumbing, not a bet. The honest caveat is dependency: a 50-person, donor-seeded operation hasn't shown the trade continues once the grants stop.

Why it matters: the adoption that lasts rarely arrives by decree — it arrives when a merchant takes a coin because the person in front of them is holding one.
