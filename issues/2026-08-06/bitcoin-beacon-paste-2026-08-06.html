Kenya and South Sudan Now Send Bitcoin Between Them for Free

NAIROBI — At a hackathon in Nairobi this summer, a handful of African bitcoin firms rewired how money crosses their borders. Tando, which already lets 40 million Kenyan M-PESA numbers receive bitcoin, tore out the Lightning plumbing it rented from a middleman and replaced it with Bark, an open-source build of the Ark protocol. The switch cut its liquidity costs by an estimated 50 to 80% — and kept every coin in the operator's own hands.

Now the same rail reaches South Sudan, where a developer built a Tando-style gateway on MTN Mobile Money. A trader in Juba and a shopkeeper in Nairobi can move value between two currencies with zero routing fees and no company holding their funds offshore. A Nigerian team is extending it to bank accounts in Lagos, Accra, and Johannesburg — together spanning five currencies and more than 400 million people.

Why it matters: the cheapest way to move money across Africa is being built in Africa, and it answers to no one off the continent.
