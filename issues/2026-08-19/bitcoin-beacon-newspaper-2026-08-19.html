Hackers Drained $115 Million From Coldcard Wallets

A flaw that shipped in 2021 let thieves guess the seed phrases behind one of bitcoin’s most trusted hardware wallets.

TORONTO — The point of a hardware wallet is that no one can guess your keys. For thousands of Coldcard owners that promise broke on July 31, when thieves began emptying wallets untouched for years. By August 16, Galaxy Research put confirmed losses above $115 million in bitcoin — and said the total could pass $130 million.

The flaw sat in the most sensitive step a wallet performs: generating the random seed. In Coldcard Mk3 firmware from March 2021 onward, seed generation quietly fell back to a weak software generator instead of the hardware one, making keys guessable from afar. Coinkite said the bug “silently went unnoticed” and grew with every release. Galaxy counts at least 15 separate attackers and 200-plus victims; the typical stolen coin had sat untouched 3.5 years. Days later, Swiss maker BitBox patched severe firmware bugs of its own.

Why it matters: self-custody removes the middleman — and the safety net.
