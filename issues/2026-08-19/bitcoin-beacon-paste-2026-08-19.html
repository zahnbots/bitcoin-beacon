Hackers Drained $115 Million From Coldcard Wallets

TORONTO — The point of a hardware wallet is that no one can guess your keys. For thousands of Coldcard owners that broke on July 31, when thieves began emptying wallets untouched for years. By August 16, Galaxy Research put confirmed losses above $115 million — and said the total could pass $130 million. The flaw sat in seed generation: Coldcard Mk3 firmware from March 2021 quietly used a weak software randomizer instead of the hardware one, making keys guessable from afar. Galaxy counts at least 15 attackers and 200-plus victims; the typical stolen coin had sat untouched 3.5 years. Days later, Swiss maker BitBox patched severe firmware bugs of its own.

Why it matters: self-custody removes the middleman — and the safety net.
