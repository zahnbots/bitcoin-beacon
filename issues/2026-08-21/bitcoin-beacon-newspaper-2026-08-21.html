Bitcoin Tops $79,000 in Its Best Week Since 2023

A Treasury plumbing change lit the fuse; a wall of crushed short bets did the rest. The move is real — the demand under it is still thin.

NEW YORK — Bitcoin climbed as high as $79,400 on Friday and is up nearly 24% since Monday, its best week since 2023. The spark was the U.S. Treasury’s move to expand its long-dated bond buybacks — not money-printing, but a debt-management shift that eases long yields and softens the dollar.

Leverage did the rest. About $3.3 billion in positions were liquidated Wednesday and another $1.24 billion over the next day, shorts making up $1.06 billion of it. Traders keep fading the move and keep being wrong; the book still leans net short. Two demand signals flicker green — spot ETFs pulled in $606 million on Aug. 20, and CryptoQuant’s spot-demand gauge is close to turning positive for the first time since February — but the gains were built on forced buying, not fresh holders.

H100’s all-share purchase of NSD is a glimpse of the next phase: treasuries merging into bitcoin holding companies. The bull case — supply locked in vehicles that never sell — is also the bear case, because it all runs on a premium to net asset value, and premiums compress. Markets & Institutions

STOCKHOLM — Sweden’s H100 Group tripled its stack to 3,506 BTC by buying Norway’s NSD entirely in stock — 790 million new shares, no cash, no coins sold. It calls the deal the first bitcoin-for-bitcoin public acquisition and now ranks as Europe’s second-largest corporate holder. The structure only works while the shares trade above the bitcoin behind them. Full story →

Network & Mining

ASUNCIÓN — Paraguay’s legal bitcoin miners fell from 71 to 41 in a year as industrial power tariffs doubled. The survivors reserve 943.8 MW, and four firms alone hold 730 of it. Fewer, bigger miners paying more have pushed the state utility’s mining revenue toward $350 million — with an AI land-grab for power and a 2027 contract cliff ahead. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States

WASHINGTON — CFTC Chairman Michael Selig told staff to build a crypto market regime using existing authority, whether or not the Senate passes the Clarity Act before its Sept. 15 cloture vote. He wants rules for leveraged trading and a legal path for DeFi. Agency rules arrive faster than a statute — and are far easier to challenge or reverse. Full story →

THIMPHU — Bhutan moved another 175 BTC (~$11.85M) to a wallet it has used before, extending a year-long drawdown of a stack that topped 13,000 BTC in 2024. Analysts tie the sales to the Gelephu megacity, toward which the kingdom pledged up to 10,000 BTC. Where El Salvador vows never to sell, Bhutan is quietly spending. Full story →

What We’re Watching

Ethiopia’s national mine. State-owned Ethiopian Investment Holdings is hunting partners for a government bitcoin-mining venture on surplus hydropower — a country that legalized mining while banning trading. The September FOMC. Futures still price a small chance of a rate hike; a surprise either way lands straight on this week’s leverage. The U.S. reserve. A White House adviser has said a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve announcement is coming “in the near term” — still unannounced.
