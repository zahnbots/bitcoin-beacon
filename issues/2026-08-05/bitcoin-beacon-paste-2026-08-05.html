A Guatemalan Lake Town Runs on Bitcoin

PANAJACHEL, Guatemala — The volcanoes ringing Lake Atitlán draw the tourists; the exchange rate keeps them. In the lakeside town Guatemalans call Pana, a hotel bill or a plate of pepián settles by scanning a QR code and sending a few thousand satoshis over Lightning — no bank in between. Locals call it Bitcoin Lake, and since 2022 it has grown into one of Central America’s more durable circular economies: roughly 80 businesses now take bitcoin, and an earlier count found about 90% of participating merchants were unbanked. The twist sits at the water’s edge — a project nicknamed Kaboom collects the used cooking oil restaurants once tipped toward the lake, burns it for power, and runs bitcoin miners on it. Waste becomes electricity; electricity earns bitcoin; bitcoin funds the collection and a My First Bitcoin class at a local school.

Why it matters: bitcoin becomes money where it solves a specific local problem — not where a government orders people to use it.
