A Guatemalan Lake Town Runs on Bitcoin

On Lake Atitlán, roughly 80 shops take bitcoin over Lightning — and a miner fed on fried-food grease turns the town’s waste oil into hashrate.

PANAJACHEL, Guatemala — The volcanoes ringing Lake Atitlán draw the tourists; the exchange rate keeps them. In the lakeside town Guatemalans call Pana, a hotel bill or a plate of pepián settles by scanning a QR code and sending a few thousand satoshis over Lightning — no bank in between.

Locals call it Bitcoin Lake. Since 2022 it has grown into one of Central America’s more durable circular economies: roughly 80 businesses now take bitcoin, and an earlier count found about 90% of participating merchants were unbanked — people transacting on a global network from a secondhand phone.

The twist sits at the water’s edge. A project nicknamed Kaboom collects the used cooking oil that restaurants once tipped toward the lake, burns it for power, and runs bitcoin miners on it. Waste becomes electricity; electricity earns bitcoin; bitcoin funds the collection and a My First Bitcoin class at a local school.

Why it matters: bitcoin becomes money where it solves a specific local problem — not where a government orders people to use it.

The Take · Opinion

Bitcoin’s Obituary Is Due Again. This Time It’s Quantum.

Bitcoin died at Mt. Gox, died each time China banned it, was going to die when Wall Street wrapped it in ETFs. This week it’s dying of quantum computers. The obituaries are well written and, so far, all early. Quantum deserves more than an eye-roll — a cryptographic break is physics, not a market panic, and a third of all coins sit in exposed addresses. But look at the two clocks: the threat runs on hardware still far from breaking a key; the fix runs on proposals that already exist and the strongest migration incentive ever built — keeping your money.

Network & Mining · New York

Q-Day, 2028. Fundstrat’s Tom Lee told CNBC a quantum computer could break bitcoin’s cryptography by 2028 and that the network hasn’t agreed how to stop it. The threat is real but early: no machine comes close, and post-quantum fixes are already in draft. His aside that Ethereum and Solana are safe is wrong — they use the same curve. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States · Washington

The architects walk. Tyler Williams, the Treasury adviser who helped build America’s Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, is resigning as the CLARITY market-structure bill stalls before the recess — one of a cluster of crypto-policy exits across Washington. The reserve’s ~328,000 seized coins aren’t going anywhere; the plan to buy more just lost its engine. Full story →

Markets & Institutions · Singapore

A miner stops hoarding. Bitdeer liquidated its entire treasury — about 1,133 coins — to zero to fund an AI pivot, becoming the largest listed self-miner holding no bitcoin. Mining margins are at record lows. The coins didn’t vanish; they moved to buyers with longer horizons, while the miner became an AI landlord. Full story →

Money & Macro · Riyadh

The Gulf’s young buyers. Saudi Arabia is the region’s fastest-growing digital-asset market, up about 153% year over year, driven by under-35s with smartphones rather than sovereigns hoarding coins. The riyal is stable, so this is discretionary demand — and it’s running well ahead of the rules meant to govern it. Full story →
