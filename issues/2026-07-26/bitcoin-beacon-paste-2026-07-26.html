Indonesia Banned Bitcoin Payments. Its Users Found a Loophole.

BANDUNG — Indonesia made paying in bitcoin illegal almost a decade ago, so its bitcoiners stopped calling it paying. Across roughly forty cities, a community now some 55,000 strong has built a national circular economy on one piece of legal jujitsu: at a member shop you don't spend your bitcoin, you redeem it — the way you'd redeem loyalty points or airline miles. Same coffee, same coin, different verb, and on the right side of a 2018 rule that still bars crypto as a means of payment.

The plumbing comes from Fedi's federated e-cash, with a federation estimated at 10,000 to 20,000 members. The classes are free, taught monetary-history-first by organizers who watched an earlier "Bitcoin Island" wave on Bali get crushed by the 2017 crackdown. The rupiah, down about 61% against the dollar over thirty years, does the recruiting.

Why it matters: adoption doesn't wait for permission — and the closed-loop workaround is a template any country with a payments ban can copy.
