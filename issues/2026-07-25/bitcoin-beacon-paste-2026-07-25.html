El Salvador Keeps Buying Bitcoin as IMF Reviews Stall

SAN SALVADOR — On the Pacific coast, a shopkeeper still takes bitcoin for a plate of pupusas and a cold bottle of water. Four years after the legal-tender law that made this country famous, the everyday scene is smaller than the headlines once promised — but the state's own stack keeps growing. The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve now holds more than 7,700 coins, worth roughly half a billion dollars, ranking El Salvador among the five largest sovereign holders on record.

That growth is the problem. Under its $1.4 billion IMF program, the government agreed to a hard ceiling of zero on new public-sector purchases. President Nayib Bukele keeps posting that the state buys one coin a day; the Fund insists the reserve is only shuffling coins between wallets it already controls. Two scheduled reviews — due last September and this March — have quietly slipped, with the next now expected before year-end.

Why it matters: the country that turned bitcoin into policy is now the test case for whether a government can hold it and satisfy its lenders at the same time.
