A Firmware Bug Drained $38 Million From Coldcard Wallets

TORONTO — Between 01:31 and 01:56 UTC Friday, someone emptied about 594 bitcoin — roughly $38 million — from some 500 wallets in a single 25-minute sweep. The victims weren’t careless. They’d done the right thing: moved coins off exchanges onto a Coldcard, one of bitcoin’s most respected hardware wallets. The betrayal came from inside the device — a firmware flaw dating to March 2021 that quietly built wallet seeds with a fraction of the randomness they needed, turning “unguessable” phrases into guessable ones. Coinkite has shipped fixed firmware, but a patch can’t repair a weak seed. Anyone who set up a Coldcard Mk3 since 2021 without their own dice entropy or a passphrase should update, make a fresh wallet, and move their coins today.

Why it matters: self-custody is still right — but its tools now face auditors that never sleep.
