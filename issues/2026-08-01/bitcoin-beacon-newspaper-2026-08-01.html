A Firmware Bug Drained $38 Million From Coldcard Wallets

Bitcoin’s most trusted hardware wallet generated weak seeds for five years. Thieves needed 25 minutes.

TORONTO — Between 01:31 and 01:56 UTC Friday, someone emptied about 594 bitcoin — roughly $38 million — from some 500 wallets in a single 25-minute sweep. The victims weren’t careless. They’d done the right thing: taken their coins off exchanges and onto a Coldcard, one of the most respected hardware wallets in bitcoin.

The betrayal came from inside the device. A firmware flaw dating to March 2021 quietly built wallet seeds with a fraction of the randomness they should have had — turning “unguessable” phrases into guessable ones. Every drained wallet was single-signature; the stolen coins span 2021 to 2026, matching the bug’s age.

Coinkite has shipped fixed firmware, but a patch can’t repair a weak seed. Anyone who set up a Coldcard Mk3 since 2021 without their own dice entropy or a passphrase should update, make a fresh wallet, and move their coins today. The unsettling part: an AI likely found the bug by reading the open-source code, and an estimated $70-million-plus bounty now hangs over every wallet codebase that touches private keys.

Why it matters: self-custody is still right — but its tools now face auditors that never sleep.

The Take · Opinion

Wall Street Wants Bitcoin to Pay Interest. Ask Where It Comes From.

Regulators just cleared bitcoin ETFs that pay a yield, and the pitch writes itself: own bitcoin, earn on it too. One question settles most of the argument — where does the yield come from? Bitcoin pays no interest; to make it pay, someone lends your coins or writes options against them. The 2022 graveyard — Celsius, BlockFi, Voyager — was built on exactly that promise. On the Ground · Austin

A public company on Lightning. Vida Global, a small Austin AI firm, began paying its global team in bitcoin over Lightning — with zero BTC on its balance sheet. Using Voltage Credit, it draws dollars, sends instant Lightning payments, and repays like a vendor invoice. The trigger wasn’t ideology: an Argentine employee asked to be paid in something the peso couldn’t erode. The CFO objection just became a template problem. Full story →

Markets & Institutions · New York

Yield comes to the ETF. The SEC cleared a path for bitcoin ETFs that generate income, approving a Cboe rule change and a no-action letter that let funds lend or write options against their coins. The CME is going 24/7 in early 2026. Wall Street keeps building bitcoin’s plumbing — and quietly re-importing the counterparty risk bitcoin was meant to remove. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States · San Salvador

An election enters the picture. El Salvador’s two main opposition parties named 2027 presidential candidates, and both call Bukele’s bitcoin strategy a fiscal failure. Neither offers a rival plan, and both start far behind a popular incumbent still buying roughly a bitcoin a day (holdings near 7,725 BTC).
