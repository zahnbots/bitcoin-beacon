In Costa Rica, Surf Towns Spend Bitcoin Over Lightning

UVITA, Costa Rica — Along the surf breaks of the southern Pacific coast, paying in bitcoin has become unremarkable. A traveler taps a phone at a Dominical café, the screen shows colones, and the money lands in the owner’s bank account seconds later. The rail is Bitcoin Jungle, a project started in the Osa region in 2021 that has wired more than 200 businesses — restaurants, surf shops, lodges, clinics — to accept bitcoin over the Lightning Network. Merchants never hold the coin; the software settles them instantly in colones. Unlike El Salvador’s Bitcoin Beach, which handed bitcoin to residents, Jungle bet on tourists who already own it and want to spend it.

Why it matters: the adoption that survives a bear market looks like a paid lunch, not a price chart.
