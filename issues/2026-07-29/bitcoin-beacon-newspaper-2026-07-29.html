In Costa Rica, Surf Towns Spend Bitcoin Over Lightning

A grassroots project called Bitcoin Jungle has wired more than 200 businesses along the Pacific coast to take bitcoin — and settle instantly in colones.

UVITA, Costa Rica — Along the surf breaks of the southern Pacific coast, paying in bitcoin has become unremarkable. A traveler taps a phone at a Dominical café, the screen shows colones, and the money lands in the owner’s bank account seconds later over SINPE, the national instant-transfer system.

The rail is Bitcoin Jungle, a project started in the Osa region in 2021 that has since wired more than 200 businesses — restaurants, surf shops, lodges, clinics — to accept bitcoin over the Lightning Network. Merchants never hold the coin; the software converts each payment to local currency instantly.

Unlike El Salvador’s Bitcoin Beach, which handed bitcoin to residents, Jungle bet on tourists who already own it and want to spend it. It runs on open-source tools and free community classes, not a government mandate.

Why it matters: the adoption that survives a bear market looks like a paid lunch, not a price chart.

Money & Macro

BUENOS AIRES. Argentina’s central bank is unwinding its 2022 crypto ban, clearing licensed banks — Galicia, BBVA, Santander — to offer bitcoin custody and trading. Separately, the market regulator now counts crypto toward the net worth that defines a “qualified investor.” In a country where roughly a quarter of adults hold crypto, the banks are chasing customers they already lost. Policy & Nation-States

ACCRA. Ghana passed its first virtual-asset law in December, and the Bank of Ghana now requires every crypto firm serving Ghanaians to register — a precursor to full licences due through 2026. About 17% of adults, some three million people, already use bitcoin and crypto, moving an estimated $3 billion a year. The state is regulating a market that grew without it. Full story →
