Russia Legalizes Bitcoin Trading, Caps Retail Buyers at $3,700 a Year

MOSCOW — Putin signed a law on Aug 4 legalizing regulated crypto trading; on Aug 11 the Bank of Russia cleared bitcoin, ether and Tether for licensed exchanges. Ordinary Russians can buy just ~300,000 rubles ($3,700) a year, after a risk test; qualified investors have no cap. Core rules start Sept 1.

Why it matters: the question is no longer whether governments allow bitcoin, but on what terms — and Russia’s terms are a cage with the door left ajar.

Riot Rents Its Bitcoin Mine to Anthropic for $9 Billion

Riot Platforms signed a 20-year lease handing 191 MW at its Rockdale, Texas campus to AI lab Anthropic — $9.1 billion base, up to $16.1 billion with extensions. The bitcoin miner becomes an energy landlord, the starkest sign yet that AI is outbidding hashing for cheap power.

Goldman Pays $2.25 Billion for a Bitcoin Income Fund

Goldman Sachs agreed to buy NEOS Investments for up to $2.25 billion, landing BTCI — a ~$1 billion fund that holds bitcoin ETPs and sells covered calls for monthly income. Goldman leapfrogs BlackRock in bitcoin yield without buying a single coin outright.

Binance Overtakes the CME in Bitcoin Futures

The cash-and-carry “basis trade” that pulled institutions into regulated CME futures has stopped paying — its premium fell below the yield on 2-year Treasuries. CME open interest hit its lowest since Feb 2024, and Binance overtook it for the first time since 2023. Liquidity is drifting back offshore.

South Korea Blocks Foreign Exchanges, Clears Its Banks to Buy In

South Korea pushed Google to pull dozens of overseas exchange apps — including OKX and Bybit — from new Android installs, then softened its 20% exchange ownership cap toward 34% so mega-banks KB and Shinhan can buy Upbit, Bithumb and Coinone. Bitcoin trading is being herded onshore, into bank hands.
