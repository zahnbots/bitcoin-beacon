Ethiopia and Paraguay Turn Spare Hydropower Into Bitcoin

Two of the world’s poorer countries are selling surplus dam electricity to bitcoin miners — and earning hard currency their exports rarely deliver.

ADDIS ABABA — The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam was built to light a nation. Some of its power is minting bitcoin instead. Ethiopia — where roughly half the population still lacks reliable electricity — has become one of the world’s fastest-growing mining hosts, selling surplus hydropower to operators who pay in dollars. State-linked deals now draw an estimated 600 megawatts across two dozen sites, worth around $55 million in a single recent year. The appeal is stranded power: Ethiopia’s dams outrun the wires that reach its homes and factories, and a miner is a container that plugs in at the turbine and wires hard currency back. Paraguay has run the same play longer, bidding miners against Brazil for Itaipú’s surplus at $0.033 a kilowatt-hour, hosted capacity up an estimated 54%. Both now weigh tariff hikes that could end the bargain.

Why it matters: for states that struggle to earn dollars, a dam plus a shipping container is starting to beat a cash crop.
