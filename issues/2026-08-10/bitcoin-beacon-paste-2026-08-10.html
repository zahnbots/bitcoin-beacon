Ethiopia and Paraguay Turn Spare Hydropower Into Bitcoin

ADDIS ABABA, Ethiopia — The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam was built to light a nation. Some of its power is minting bitcoin instead. Ethiopia — where roughly half the population still lacks reliable electricity — has become one of the world’s fastest-growing mining hosts, selling surplus hydropower to operators who pay in dollars: an estimated 600 megawatts across two dozen sites, worth around $55 million in a single recent year. Paraguay runs the same play at Itaipú, bidding miners against Brazil for power at $0.033 a kilowatt-hour.

Why it matters: for states that struggle to earn dollars, a dam plus a shipping container is starting to beat a cash crop.

Miners Ignored a Fork Built to Ban Ordinals

A soft fork built to purge Ordinals data, BIP-110, hit its activation block with 2.53% miner support — 51 of 2,016 blocks, far under the 55% threshold. The breakaway chain stalled within hours. A reminder that Bitcoin’s rules change only when miners and nodes run the code, not when a faction shouts. Full story →
