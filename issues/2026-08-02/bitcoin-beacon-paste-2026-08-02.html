Square Will Let Four Million Shops Take Bitcoin

SAN FRANCISCO — Bitcoin's biggest merchant test isn't a village or a beach town. It's the point-of-sale terminal on your corner store's counter. Block has switched on Lightning bitcoin acceptance across Square — the payment system behind millions of American small businesses — with no new hardware, a QR code at checkout, and dollars in the till by default.

More than a million sellers can already take it; the company is opening the door to its full base of over four million. The lure is the price: zero processing fees on bitcoin payments through 2026, then a flat 1% — roughly a third of what cards skim. The shopkeeper keeps more of the sale; the customer spends sats without the merchant ever touching a wallet. Whether shoppers pay this way is the open question — most will still tap a card. But the rail is now installed at the scale where habits form.

Why it matters: adoption stops being a movement and becomes a default when the country's biggest checkout counter quietly supports it.
