SAN FRANCISCO — Bitcoin’s biggest merchant test isn’t a village or a beach town. It’s the point-of-sale terminal on your corner store’s counter. Block has switched on Lightning bitcoin acceptance across Square — the payment system behind millions of American small businesses — with no new hardware, a QR code at checkout, and dollars in the till by default.

More than a million sellers can already take it; the company is opening the door to its full base of over four million. The lure is the price: zero processing fees on bitcoin payments through 2026, then a flat 1% — roughly a third of what cards skim. The shopkeeper keeps more of the sale; the customer spends sats without the merchant ever touching a wallet.

Whether shoppers pay this way is the open question — most will still tap a card. But the rail is now installed at the scale where habits form.

Why it matters: adoption stops being a movement and becomes a default when the country’s biggest checkout counter quietly supports it.

The Take · Opinion

Governments Want Bitcoin. They Just Don’t Want You Holding It.

Three states moved on bitcoin this month, and none of them trusted a citizen with a key. Kazakhstan will pocket a tenth of what its licensed miners produce. Bhutan handed its national coins to a Toronto asset manager. Oman routes its miners through a mandatory state pool. Each is “bitcoin adoption,” and each inverts the point: a bearer asset, re-wrapped in a custodian. The state has learned it can hold the hardest money ever made — and still stand between you and it.

Network & Mining · Astana

A tithe on the hashrate. A rule that took effect August 1 lets licensed Kazakh miners buy power at capped 10-year rates from the Ekibastuz coal plant — in exchange for handing 10% of the bitcoin they mine to a new national reserve. Only farms above 150 MW qualify. Kazakhstan is turning its grid into a sovereign bitcoin-accumulation machine, paid for by the miners it hosts. Full story →

Markets & Institutions · Thimphu

The first sovereign to hire a manager. Bhutan’s Gelephu Mindfulness City handed part of its bitcoin treasury to 3iQ, a Toronto asset manager — the first time a bitcoin-holding state has publicly outsourced custody and strategy to a regulated outside firm. The kingdom mined its stack itself; now it wants professionals to run it. The size of the tranche is undisclosed. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States · Dar es Salaam

From warning to rulebook. The Bank of Tanzania has finished a regulatory blueprint for digital assets, seven years after telling citizens to stay away. It covers exchanges, stablecoins and virtual assets, and follows a 3% transaction tax and a May stablecoin sandbox. Nothing is law yet — but East Africa’s next bitcoin market is being drawn on paper.
