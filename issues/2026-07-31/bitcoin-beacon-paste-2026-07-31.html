In Zambia, a Bitcoin Mine Keeps the Lights On

ZENGAMINA, Zambia — On a bend of the Zambezi in Zambia's remote north-west, a hydro dam built two decades ago with $3 million in charity money was running short of paying customers. Too few families nearby could afford its power to keep it solvent. In 2022, a shipping container packed with 120 bitcoin miners arrived and began buying the surplus.

Today the plant serves more than 15,000 people, and the mine is its buyer of last resort. Gridless — backed by Jack Dorsey's Block — runs six such sites across Zambia, Malawi and Kenya, each parked at a rural mini-grid with more power than the local economy can absorb. In one Malawian village, the extra revenue wired up 500 more homes.

Why it matters: bitcoin mining is turning stranded rural electricity into a reason to keep the lights on.

Indonesia Hands Its Bitcoin Market to the Financial Regulator
