In Zambia, a Bitcoin Mine Keeps the Lights On

A charity-built hydro dam on the Zambezi was failing. Then bitcoin miners moved in — and 15,000 people kept their power.

ZENGAMINA, Zambia — On a bend of the Zambezi in Zambia’s remote north-west, a hydro dam built two decades ago with $3 million in charity money was running short of paying customers. Too few families nearby could afford its electricity to keep it solvent. In 2022, a shipping container packed with 120 bitcoin miners arrived and began buying the unused power.

Today the plant serves more than 15,000 people, and the mine is its buyer of last resort. The operator, Gridless — backed by Jack Dorsey’s Block — runs six such sites across Zambia, Malawi and Kenya, each parked at a rural mini-grid with more power than the local economy can absorb. In one Malawian village, the extra revenue let the station wire up 500 more homes.

The rigs are mobile by design: when Zengamina joins Zambia’s national grid, expected within a year, the miners pack up and move on.

Why it matters: bitcoin mining is turning stranded rural electricity into a reason to keep the lights on.

The Take

Big Investors Own Bitcoin They Can’t Spend

Norway’s wealth fund holds roughly 11,400 bitcoin indirectly. Abu Dhabi’s Mubadala counts a BlackRock bitcoin ETF among its largest positions. Luxembourg became the first eurozone state to route 1% of its fund into one.

All of it sits inside regulated wrappers whose keys belong to a custodian — exposure to a price, not possession of an asset. In Lagos and rural Zambia, people hold the coin itself: spendable, and seizable by no one. Two very different things now share a ticker.

Money & Macro

LAGOS — The naira has slid from about ₦750 to the dollar in 2023 to near ₦1,600 today, erasing more than 40% of the purchasing power of anyone who held only cash. Nigeria now counts an estimated 27–30 million crypto users — most young and low-income — parking savings in bitcoin and dollar-pegged tokens the naira can’t match. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States

BUDAPEST — Hungary’s parliament repealed a 2024 rule — stricter than the EU’s own — that forced third-party “validators” to verify the origin of crypto before any conversion. Days earlier, the central bank handed CoinCash operator Tiwala Solutions the country’s first licence under the EU’s MiCA regime. One of the bloc’s crypto holdouts just chose to align. Full story →

JAKARTA — An amended financial-sector law moved oversight of crypto from Indonesia’s commodities agency to its financial regulator, the OJK, effective mid-June. The cost of running an exchange jumped: new licences require about 500 billion rupiah (~$320 million) in capital, with a July 1 compliance deadline. Jakarta is treating bitcoin as finance, not a commodity. Full story →
