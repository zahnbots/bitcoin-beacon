A Chinese Insurer Swapped Shares for a Bitcoin Treasury

No dollars changed hands — and the investors who paid in bitcoin are taking the boardroom with them.

SHENZHEN — A Chinese insurance-technology firm has built a bitcoin treasury without spending a dollar to do it. Zhibao Technology, listed on the Nasdaq, agreed on July 31 to sell $154.7 million of new stock — and to take payment in bitcoin, 2,380 coins, valued at a fixed $65,000 each.

No cash was raised and none was spent buying on the open market. The bitcoin simply moved onto the balance sheet as the price of the shares — one of the cleanest ways yet to stand up a corporate treasury overnight.

The catch is control. The deal shrinks Zhibao’s board to five seats and pushes out four directors, the chief executive and the chief financial officer, handing the incoming investors the run of the company. The founder keeps his seat and is seeking veto power. The coins, Zhibao says, will fund artificial-intelligence work in its insurance arm.

Why it matters: a company can now acquire bitcoin faster than it can raise the money to buy it — but the shortcut can cost it the boardroom.

The Take · Opinion

Bitcoin’s Biggest Miners Are Becoming AI Landlords

Core Scientific just signed a 15-year, $14-billion deal to rent its power and buildings to AMD for artificial intelligence — and to wind its bitcoin mining down. TeraWulf leased $19 billion of capacity to Anthropic; CleanSpark is pivoting too. The megawatts that secured the network are being re-let to chatbots, because AI pays more per watt than hashing does. Rational for the miners. A quieter question for the network.

Network & Mining · Johor

The grid pays the bill. Police in Malaysia’s Johor state seized 71 machines from four rented units where a crew had mined bitcoin for a month on electricity siphoned past the meter — about $16,600 stolen in weeks. It is a sliver of the problem: Malaysia has tied roughly 14,000 properties to mining-linked power theft since 2020, and Thailand and Indonesia are raiding too. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States · Washington

Censorship-resistance, both ways. Tehran made ships buy Strait of Hormuz passage insurance priced in bitcoin to dodge Western banks. On July 31 the US Treasury blacklisted the two firms behind it, plus eight shadow-fleet vessels. Bitcoin let the scheme skip the banking system — it could not hide the scheme from a sanctions list. Money & Macro · Global

The quiet squeeze. Public companies scooped up about 110,000 bitcoin in the second quarter — nearly double the prior two combined, and more than twice what miners dug up over the stretch. Corporate holdings have crossed 1.26 million coins, over 6% of all bitcoin. One catch: a single firm, Strategy, holds roughly 70% of that pile. Full story →
