Chipper Cash Now Moves Half Its Bitcoin on Lightning

One of Africa’s largest fintechs quietly turned Lightning into a mass-market rail — and crossed the line where most of its bitcoin no longer touches the base chain.

KAMPALA — Chipper Cash didn’t announce anything. It crossed a line in its own ledger: more than half of every bitcoin transaction on the platform now settles over the Lightning Network instead of the base chain. For a fintech with seven million users across Africa, that makes it one of the largest real-world Lightning deployments anywhere.

The base layer confirms a block every ten minutes and charges a fee sized to global demand — fine for large settlements, useless for sending a few dollars to a cousin in the next country. Lightning routes those small, frequent payments in a second for a fraction of a cent, running through infrastructure firm Voltage so the app never manages channels itself. Chipper launched in 2018 to move money across African borders banks treat as walls; bitcoin is now one of the pipes value travels through. The catch: it’s all custodial — Chipper holds the keys.

Why it matters: the share of a mainstream app’s bitcoin moving over Lightning measures use, not speculation — and by that metric, in one of the world’s hardest markets, bitcoin just became infrastructure.

The Take

Custodial Bitcoin Apps Aren’t a Betrayal. They’re the On-Ramp.

“Not your keys, not your coins” is bitcoin’s founding rule — and the wrong first lesson for the next billion users. The apps that onboarded millions, from Cash App to Chivo to Chipper, are mostly custodial, and purists call that a betrayal. But the real alternative for a first-time saver isn’t a hardware wallet; it’s no bitcoin at all. Custodial is the ladder’s first rung — provided the app makes climbing off it easy. The counter, written in the losses at Celsius, FTX and this week’s Poolin bankruptcy, is that a custodian is a single point of failure. Both are true.

Money & Macro

ISTANBUL. Turkey grew its crypto activity about 7% to roughly $40 billion early this year, climbing to fifth worldwide — the only major market to expand. The driver isn’t speculation but a sliding lira: with the currency melting, bitcoin and dollar stablecoins read as savings. In March, Ankara floated a 0.003% trade tax and a 10% levy on gains. Markets & Institutions

SINGAPORE. Poolin, which coordinated nearly a fifth of Bitcoin’s hashrate in 2019, filed for bankruptcy owing 11,700 users $164 million — balances frozen since a 2022 liquidity crisis. Its West Texas sites go up for about $52 million, near 32 cents on the dollar. The final trigger was 2026’s problem: AI now outbids miners for cheap power. Full story →

Policy & Nation-States

HANOI. Vietnam has cleared five firms toward its first licensed exchanges, moving bitcoin trading onshore for one of the world’s most active markets. The bar is steep — roughly $400 million in charter capital and 65% institutional ownership — so the first venues will be few. Which assets they may list isn’t yet published. Full story →

Network & Mining

ADDIS ABABA. Cheap hydropower from the new Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam drew the world’s miners; now the utility is raising their tariff 24% this month, part of a three-year climb from about four cents a kilowatt-hour toward 6.5. By its own estimate, half of Ethiopia’s mines turn unprofitable by mid-2026. Insiders call it a “Kazakhstan moment.” Full story →
