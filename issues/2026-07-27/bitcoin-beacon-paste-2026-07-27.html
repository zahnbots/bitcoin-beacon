Chipper Cash Now Moves Half Its Bitcoin on Lightning

KAMPALA — Chipper Cash didn't announce anything. It crossed a line in its own ledger: more than half of every bitcoin transaction on the platform now settles over the Lightning Network instead of the base chain. For a fintech with seven million users across Africa, that makes it one of the largest real-world Lightning deployments anywhere.

The base layer charges a fee sized to global demand — fine for large settlements, useless for sending a few dollars across a border. Lightning routes those small, frequent payments in a second for a fraction of a cent, run through infrastructure firm Voltage so the app never manages channels itself. The catch: it's all custodial — Chipper holds the keys.

Why it matters: the share of a mainstream app's bitcoin moving over Lightning measures use, not speculation — and by that metric, in one of the world's hardest markets, bitcoin just became infrastructure.
