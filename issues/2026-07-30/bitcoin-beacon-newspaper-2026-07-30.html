As the Rial Collapses, Iranians Move to Bitcoin

A currency that has shed a third of its value in seven months is teaching ten million Iranians to hold savings no bank can freeze.

TEHRAN — The rial has lost a third of its value since New Year’s, sliding past 1.95 million to the dollar in late July. The IMF projects inflation near 69% this year; food prices have roughly doubled. For an Iranian whose wage buys less every week, a bank account is a slow leak.

So millions are opting out. An estimated ten million people — about a fifth of adults — now hold bitcoin or other crypto, despite a state that restricts mining and stablecoins alike. The appeal is self-custody: coins the government cannot freeze, print, or convert overnight, the way Lebanon’s banks once trapped their depositors.

Bitcoin is not spreading here because Iranians read Satoshi. It is spreading because the alternative is watching savings evaporate.

Why it matters: the strongest case for bitcoin is rarely made by its advocates — it is made by a failing currency.

The Take

The Fed Meets This Week. Bitcoin’s New Users Won’t Notice.

The FOMC gathers this week and every bitcoin desk in Manhattan will parse the wording. Rate up, the ETFs bleed; rate steady, the leverage piles back on. That is the bitcoin of Wall Street — a risk asset priced against the dollar.

It is not the bitcoin of Lagos, Buenos Aires or Tehran. There, no one is handicapping a September hike. They are handicapping a currency that lost a third of its value while the Fed debated a quarter-point. Two assets share a ticker and little else — and only one is growing because people need it.

Policy & Nation-States

TOKYO. Japan made bitcoin a financial product. On July 15 the Diet folded bitcoin and roughly a hundred other assets under its securities law, clearing the path to cut the tax on gains from as high as 55% to a flat 20% and to list spot bitcoin ETFs. The tax break may wait until 2028, but the legal status changed now. Full story →

Markets & Institutions

NEW YORK. The ETF money is trickling back. US spot bitcoin funds logged a third straight week of net inflows — their first such run since May — with BlackRock’s IBIT taking $319M of one week’s $499M before the streak snapped on an outflow day. GLOBAL. Lightning Labs wants software to pay in bitcoin. Its new Wavelength toolkit (July 21) lets any app — or an AI agent — hold and send self-custodial bitcoin through a simple API at one basis point, against 200–300 on card rails. Machines can now pay machines in sats. Full story →

What We’re Watching

Brazil’s reserve bill: PL 4501/2024 (RESBit), which would authorize buying up to one million BTC, is still grinding through Chamber committees.
