BlackRock Cuts Its Bitcoin-to-ETF Minimum From $25M to $1M

NEW YORK — BlackRock has cut the price of moving bitcoin into its IBIT exchange-traded fund without selling it. The minimum for an “in-kind” conversion — handing over actual bitcoin and receiving fund shares, no cash changing hands and no taxable sale triggered — drops from $25 million to $1 million, a 96% cut announced by digital-assets head Robbie Mitchnick on August 10. At the old threshold this was a Wall Street trading-desk tool; at $1 million it reaches registered advisers and family offices holding appreciated coins who want ETF convenience without the tax bill. Mitchnick says the threshold will keep falling.

Why it matters: the cheapest path between owning bitcoin and owning a bitcoin fund just got twenty-five times wider — and it runs toward Wall Street's custody.

Washington Gives Itself Until September to Define Bitcoin

The White House has given the Senate until September 15 to advance the CLARITY Act, which would classify bitcoin as a commodity under the CFTC. Adviser Patrick Witt: if they “can't get there by September 15, they never will.” Democrats blocked a pre-recess vote; a cloture motion now needs seven of them to cross. Luke Dashjr Steps Away From Ocean to Fight for a Failing Fork

Dashjr has taken a sabbatical as chairman and CTO of the Jack Dorsey-backed mining pool Ocean, turning to the stalled BIP-110 fork. His breakaway chain has managed just two blocks since splitting at block 961,632. Its developers now plan a hard fork that would abandon Bitcoin's proof-of-work for a new algorithm, picked by a secret commit-reveal. Full story →

BlackRock's New Canadian Fund Holds 3% Bitcoin by Default

BlackRock's Canadian arm listed the iShares Equity + Bitcoin ETF Portfolio (IBQT) on the Toronto exchange: 97% global stocks, 3% bitcoin, a 0.22% fee.
