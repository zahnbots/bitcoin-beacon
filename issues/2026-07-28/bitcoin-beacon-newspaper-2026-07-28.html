South Africa Puts Bitcoin at the Grocery Till

A Stellenbosch startup quietly wired the country’s checkout counters to bitcoin — and merchants never have to touch the coin.

CAPE TOWN — At a Pick n Pay till in the Western Cape, paying with bitcoin now looks like paying with anything else. The shopper opens a wallet app, scans the QR code on the card machine, and approves the rand amount before the receipt prints.

The plumbing belongs to MoneyBadger, a Stellenbosch company that bridges South Africa’s existing point-of-sale rails to bitcoin and the Lightning Network. In April the exchange Bybit wired its pay feature into that network, and the reach is now national: more than 1,500 Pick n Pay stores plus hundreds of thousands of smaller merchants reachable through Scan to Pay, Zapper and other gateways.

Merchants never hold bitcoin — they settle instantly in rand — which is exactly why they say yes. Roughly one in eleven South Africans already owns crypto, and about half of them have spent it on something.

Why it matters: adoption sticks when spending bitcoin is boring, not when it’s a statement.

Policy & Nation-States

ISLAMABAD — Pakistan has turned its temporary crypto regulator into a permanent one. The Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority, made law under the Virtual Assets Act of 2026, just closed public consultation on a licensing rulebook with ten categories of provider and a no-objection-certificate path to a full licence. For an estimated 40 million users, the exchanges where most first buy bitcoin are moving onshore and under supervision. Full story →

GLOBAL — After nine years, the BitMart exchange is winding down. It halted new sign-ups and fiat deposits on July 26; spot and futures trading end August 26, with withdrawals open until January 2027. Its BMX token fell 81% in a week. It joins a long 2026 casualty list — one tracker counts 99 dead crypto projects this year. Full story →
