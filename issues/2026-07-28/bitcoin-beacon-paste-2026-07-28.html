South Africa Puts Bitcoin at the Grocery Till

CAPE TOWN — At a Pick n Pay till in the Western Cape, paying with bitcoin now looks like paying with anything else: open a wallet app, scan the QR code on the card machine, approve the rand amount. The plumbing belongs to MoneyBadger, a Stellenbosch startup that bridges South Africa’s existing point-of-sale rails to bitcoin and the Lightning Network. In April the exchange Bybit wired its pay feature into that network, and the reach is now national: 1,500+ Pick n Pay stores plus hundreds of thousands of smaller merchants via Scan to Pay, Zapper and other gateways.

Merchants never hold bitcoin — they settle instantly in rand — which is exactly why they say yes. Roughly one in eleven South Africans owns crypto, and about half of them have spent it.

Why it matters: adoption sticks when spending bitcoin is boring, not when it’s a statement.
