Bitcoin Surges Past $72,000 as the Treasury Doubles Bond Buybacks

One plumbing decision in Washington flushed $1.4 billion in short bets and drove bitcoin to its highest level since June.

WASHINGTON — Bitcoin climbed more than 12% in a day, briefly clearing $72,000 for the first time since June, after the U.S. Treasury said it would double its long-dated bond buybacks to at least $4 billion per operation from September 9. The plan pulled the 30-year yield off a 2007 high near 5.34%, softened the dollar, and pushed money back into risk.

On leveraged venues the rally detonated roughly $1.4 billion of short positions in about an hour — a forced-buying cascade that turned a policy headline into a violent squeeze. Ether and crypto-linked stocks rose with it; a White House meeting with industry executives the same day added fuel.

Why it matters: bitcoin’s biggest moves now answer to Washington’s debt plumbing, not to any new buyer of the coin.

The Take

Bitcoin Adoption Is Built, Not Decreed

Five years ago El Salvador made bitcoin legal tender and waited for a country to spend it. It never did — barely 1% of a $5 billion remittance market runs on crypto. The lesson isn’t that bitcoin failed; it’s that a law can’t manufacture demand. Adoption that sticks is earned one merchant at a time, not gazetted from a palace.

Policy & Nation-States

SAN SALVADOR. El Salvador’s Bitcoin Law turned five this summer, and President Nayib Bukele conceded to Time that “bitcoin did not receive the widespread adoption we expected.” Crypto settled just $35.4 million of over $5 billion in first-half remittances; mandatory acceptance was scrapped last year for an IMF loan. What remains is a 7,677-coin state reserve. Full story →

SHANGHAI. A Shanghai digital-insurance broker, Zhibao, closed a $154.7 million bitcoin-funded placement and now holds 2,380 BTC — the 33rd-largest corporate stack, ahead of Core Scientific. The twist: China bans crypto trading at home, so the coins sit inside a Nasdaq-listed shell. Full story →
